After leaving Berkeley, I spent the next three months at Notre Dame, with a short trip to CMU in between for an event hosted by HCII. And now, finally, I’m back in Seattle.

So much has happened over these past three months. Everything and everyone I encountered this summer made me feel incredibly warm and supported.

The new policies for international students have constantly made things feel a little suffering. In a time filled with so much uncertainty, I often find myself thinking about what actually stays unchanged. One answer that I’ve found somewhat satisfying is the close connections between people. Our shared pursuit of good and beautiful things is what keeps us connected to one another.

During my second meeting with my advisor, he told me that I should do a little less work over the summer and spend more time having fun. He also told me to think seriously about whether I really want to do a PhD, and what kind of research I actually want to pursue. Maybe figuring those things out is more important than finishing another project or getting another paper submitted. I don’t think I’ve completely figured out the “assignment” he gave me, but at least I’ve had some thoughts of my own.

It’s always surprisingly hard to sit down and write something like this. This summer, I also kept coming back to another question: as part of an AI-native generation, how should I approach all the different courses I take at school? And how do I do research that doesn’t turn into AI slop?

I don’t really have a clear answer to any of these questions.

And maybe there shouldn’t be one.
